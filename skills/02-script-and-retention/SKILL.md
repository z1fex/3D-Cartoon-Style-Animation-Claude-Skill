# 2 · Script and retention

Owner's rule (round 1): a script that only lists facts does not keep a viewer. v1 opened on "You're sitting under an apple tree" and had no reason to stay. v2 opened on a claim, and that is the structure below.

## The seven beats

| Beat | Share of a 30 s film | Job | Example (gravity) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hook** | 0–2 s | A bold claim or question that opens a loop. Interrupt the scroll. | "Everything you know about this apple is wrong." |
| **2. Early payoff** | 2–4 s | A small surprise that proves the hook is real and buys trust. | "It probably never hit Newton's head." |
| **3. Stakes** | 4–10 s | Why it mattered, told as the viewer's own experience; concrete names, ages, places. | "1666: plague shuts his university, so Isaac, 23, is stuck on the family farm." |
| **4. The question** | 10–13 s | The question the viewer now asks too. | "Why straight down? Why never sideways?" |
| **5. Escalate** | 13–19 s | Each beat raises the scale. The viewer thinks the story is done. | "The same pull reaches the Moon, 240,000 miles away." |
| **6. Re-hook and twist** | 19–28 s | A new tension spike as attention dips, then the twist as one literal picture. | "For 250 years, that was gravity. Then Einstein rewrote it. There is no pull. … like a bowling ball on a trampoline." |
| **7. Loop** | last 2–3 s | Repeat or echo the hook so the end runs into the start. | "So yes: everything you know about this apple is wrong." |

For a 50–60 s film keep the same beats and add a second escalation or a second gag before the re-hook.

## Rules
- **Length:** about 3.3 spoken words a second after pause tightening (100 words ≈ 30 s; 165 words ≈ 50 s).
- **One idea per clause**, because every clause is a shot. Commas and full stops are your shot list.
- **Say numbers out loud**, in words the voice will read well ("two hundred and forty thousand miles"); the caption shows the numerals.
- **Never open on scene-setting.** The first line is the claim; the scene comes in beat 3.
- **Short words, active verbs, second person.** "Plague shuts his university", not "the university was closed due to plague".
- **The loop line** should work as a first line too.

## Sourcing
Every claim gets a row in the script's source table: the claim, where it comes from, and a note. When sources disagree, hedge in plain words ("probably", "about", "as tall as a house"), and never state more than the sources do. Example rows from the gravity film:

| Claim | Basis | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The apple fell; Newton asked why it falls straight down | Stukeley, *Memoirs of Sir Isaac Newton's Life* (1752), from a 1726 conversation | No account says it hit his head, so "probably never hit". |
| 1666, at home because of the plague | Conduitt's notes (1726); University of York | Accounts vary 1665–66; 1666 is the usual date. |
| About 240,000 miles to the Moon | NASA: average 238,855 miles | Said as "about". |
| For 250 years | 1666 to 1915 is 249 years | Rounded. |

## Writing the hook question for the title
At the same time, write the **topic question** that goes at the top of the frame for the first 2–3 s (skill 11): a short question that names the topic, with one key word: "How was *gravity* found?", "Why is the sky *blue?*", "How fast is a *sneeze?*". The hook line (voice) and the title question (text) work together: the title says what the video is about; the voice says why you should stay.
