# Round 1: How gravity was found (about 30 s)

**Brief from the owner (26 Oct 2026):**
- **Topic:** "the famous apple falling from the tree … how gravity was found".
- **Characters:** Newton's apple, plus Einstein.
- **Look:** skin tones, with the brand colours in the clothes and the environment.

**Angle:** Newton's apple (1666), then the turn: Einstein (1915) says gravity isn't a pull at all. The last line loops back into the first.

## Voiceover text (sent to ElevenLabs)

> You're sitting under an apple tree, and an apple drops right beside you. It's sixteen sixty-six. You're Isaac Newton, you're twenty-three, and you ask: why straight down? Why not sideways? Your answer: the Earth pulls it. And that same pull reaches the Moon, about two hundred and forty thousand miles away, and keeps it circling. But here's the weird part. In nineteen fifteen, Albert Einstein says gravity isn't a pull at all. Mass bends space and time, like a bowling ball on a trampoline, and the apple just rolls down the dent… right beside you, sitting under an apple tree.

## Claims and sources

| Claim | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| An apple fell; Newton asked why it falls straight down, not sideways | William Stukeley, *Memoirs of Sir Isaac Newton's Life* (1752), from a conversation in 1726. The Royal Society manuscript reads: "Why should that apple always descend perpendicularly to the ground… Why should it not go sideways, or upwards?" ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/whats-the-real-story-with-newton-and-the-apple-see-for-yourself), [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna34944738)) | No account says it hit his head, so the apple lands **beside** him. |
| 1666, at home because of the plague | John Conduitt's notes (1726) place it in 1666 at Woolsthorpe, after the plague closed Cambridge ([University of York](https://york.ac.uk/physics/about/newtonsappletree), [physics.info](https://physics.info/gravitation/quote-newton.shtml)) | Accounts vary (1665–66), and 1666 is the usual date. |
| Newton was 23 | Born 25 Dec 1642 (Julian calendar), so 23 for most of 1666 | |
| The same pull reaches the Moon | Conduitt: "Why not as high as the Moon," he said to himself ([physics.info](https://physics.info/gravitation/quote-newton.shtml)) | |
| About 240,000 miles to the Moon | NASA gives an average Earth–Moon distance of about 238,855 miles (384,400 km) | Said as "about". |
| 1915: Einstein's gravity isn't a pull | General relativity was presented to the Prussian Academy in November 1915 | |
| Mass bends space and time; bowling ball on a trampoline | The standard "rubber sheet" picture of curved spacetime | An analogy, not literal. |
